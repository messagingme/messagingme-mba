Guides WhatsApp opt-in: collect, prove and respect consent.

Where to ask your customers for consent, how to word it, what to write in the first message, and how to prove it the day someone asks.

Updated on 24 November 2026

What is WhatsApp opt-in?

WhatsApp opt-in is a person’s explicit consent to receive your business’s messages on WhatsApp. Without it, no campaign.

It’s a legal obligation, a Meta rule, and the condition for a channel that stays read: a customer who asked for nothing blocks or reports your number, and Meta takes it into account in that number’s quality.

What the law and Meta say

GDPR. In Europe, marketing consent is collected channel by channel: consent given for email or SMS does not cover WhatsApp. You are the data controller: it’s up to you to collect this consent and be able to prove it.

Meta. Its messaging policy requires that the person gave you their number and agreed to be contacted by you on WhatsApp. So the rule of thumb is simple: explicit consent for WhatsApp, and a record of it.

This guide is not legal advice: for a specific case, your DPO decides.

Where to collect opt-in

  • On your website: a WhatsApp-specific checkbox in a form, or the WhatsApp bubble, where the customer writes to you first.
  • In your app or customer account, alongside the other contact preferences.
  • By SMS or email: a message offering to switch to WhatsApp, with a “YES” reply.
  • In store or at the counter: a QR code that opens the conversation.
  • Through a Click-to-WhatsApp ad: the person who clicks writes to you first.

When a customer writes to you first, you can reply freely for 24 hours. To send them marketing messages later, ask for their consent in the conversation.

Writing a clear opt-in

A good opt-in says three things: the channel (WhatsApp), the kind of messages (offers, order tracking, reminders), and how to unsubscribe. The box is never pre-ticked.

“I agree to receive offers and news from [Brand] on WhatsApp. I can unsubscribe at any time by replying STOP.”

The first message: remind people of their consent

The first message reminds the person why they receive it and gives them an easy way out. Since you write first, it’s a Meta-approved template.

“Hello Camille, you agreed to receive our offers on WhatsApp when you placed your order. To stop receiving them, reply STOP.”

“You receive our offers by SMS. Would you like to receive them on WhatsApp? Reply YES to confirm.”

If a customer declines WhatsApp, keep their preferences for the other channels, unless they ask not to be contacted at all.

Prove consent and respect STOP

Keep for each contact the date, the source (form, store, conversation) and the accepted text. In Messaging Me, each contact record carries its marketing consent and the source of that consent.

A contact who replies STOP is no longer contacted: the check runs before every send, whether it comes from a campaign, a scenario, the AI agent or your team. Unsubscribes are kept for three years, so you never write again to someone who said stop.

Costly mistakes

  • Importing an SMS or email list and launching a WhatsApp campaign without consent for WhatsApp.
  • Marking a whole file as consenting “by default”: in Messaging Me, the opt-in box of an import also resubscribes people who had replied STOP. Import your contacts, step by step.
  • A promotional first message with no reminder of consent and no opt-out.
  • Classifying a promotional message as utility: Meta reclassifies it as marketing.

What Messaging Me does

  • Consent is carried by each contact record, and a marketing campaign only targets the contacts who gave it.
  • STOP is checked before every send, and HubSpot learns it on its own when it’s connected.
  • Your data is hosted in the European Union, and Messaging Me is your processor (GDPR article 28).

Frequently asked questions.

Is an SMS opt-in enough to send WhatsApp messages?

No. Marketing consent is collected channel by channel. Collect consent for WhatsApp, and remind people of it in your first message.

Can you reply to a customer who writes to you without opt-in?

Yes. When a customer writes to you, you can reply freely for 24 hours. To send them marketing messages later, ask for their consent.

How do you prove WhatsApp consent?

Keep the date, the source and the accepted text for each contact. In Messaging Me, consent and its source live on the contact record.

What happens when a customer replies STOP?

They are no longer contacted. Messaging Me checks the unsubscribe before every send and keeps it for three years.
